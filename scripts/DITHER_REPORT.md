# Dither report — film stills

Generated by `npm run stills` (`scripts/process-stills.ts`). Sources: the twelve
`slug` + `youtubeId` pairs in `content/films.json`, read at run time. Comparison
sheets live in `screenshots/dither/` (gitignored; regenerate with
`npm run stills -- --sheets`); the sheets the decision rests on are copied into
`docs/` for the record (list at the end).

Written 2026-09-07 for the remediation brief (Part 3.1 Move 2, Part 4.6, Part 5
Agent B). The previous pass chose ordered Bayer 8×8; this pass re-decided from
scratch against the brief's four questions.

## Recommendation

**Rotated clustered-dot halftone screen, 2 tones (base `#0B0D0F` + carolina
`#4B9CD3`), working width 640, dot pitch 8 working px (16 px at 1280) at 45°,
contrast 1.15, gamma 1.3, normalise on, no posterize, lossless webp, per-slug
phase offset.** These are the script defaults; a plain `npm run stills` writes
exactly the committed set.

What the treatment does, in order: extract the 16:9 frame → resize to 640×360 →
grayscale → histogram normalise → contrast 1.15 around mid grey → gamma 1.3 →
threshold each pixel against a 45° dot lattice (PostScript's "Euclidean" spot
function: a round carolina dot grows from each cell centre until the dots touch
at 50%, after which a round base hole shrinks toward the corners) → paint with the
two palette colours → nearest-neighbour upscale to 1280×720 so every working pixel
is a crisp 2×2 block.

## The three approaches

All three were rendered for every available still with the same tone curve
(normalise, contrast 1.15, gamma 1.3), two tones, and looked at as (a) whole
frames at 2× nearest (`approach-*.png`), (b) 1:1 windows of the 1280 file
(`finalists-hero.png`), (c) the 1280 file resampled to the 768 px hero screen
(`finalists-heroview.png`), and (d) the native rendition resampled to a 400 px
card (`finalists-card.png`). Each approach runs at its own best integer-cell
resolution: Bayer and Floyd–Steinberg at 320 (4 px cells; at 640 they read as a
smooth photograph, the previous pass's finding, confirmed), the halftone at 640
(2 px blocks; at 320 a dot has to be drawn in 4 px blocks and comes out as a
cross, see `halftone-sweep.png` columns 1–2).

| | Bayer 8×8 (prior) | Floyd–Steinberg | Halftone (chosen) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Printed matter vs photographic | Reads as a *computer* dither: a rigid crosshatch. It is the texture of every retro-dither shader and 1-bit Mac graphic, which is a video-game reference, not a print one. | Reads as a photograph through a fax: organic grain that preserves tone. The most photographic of the three. | Reads as a screen print or newsprint: round dots on a 45° screen, the crossover from dots to holes visible in the midtones. Unambiguously printed matter, which is the register the Criterion/A24 references share. |
| Flattening well-lit vs badly-lit sources (9.2) | Moderate. The grid is the same everywhere, but tonal detail survives inside it, so a-newby and how-does-it-feel still read as the well-lit frames. | Weakest. Every source keeps its photographic character; the set reads as eleven photocopies of photographs of different quality. | Strongest. The dot lattice imposes one structure on every frame; a-newby, how-does-it-feel and spaghetti-and-me become the same kind of object, differing only in how much of the frame is dots. |
| Legibility of faces | Good at every size. | Best at every size. | Good at card size and at the 768 px hero at pitch 16 (fdoc's eyes and mouth read, silenced's hat and gesture read); coarser pitches (20, 24, 32 px) lose fdoc's face at the hero, which is why the pitch is 16 and not 20. |
| Card (~340–460 px) vs hero (~768–1280 px) | Card: the 4 px cells at ~1 px per cell moiré unless the native 320 rendition is served and smoothly upscaled (the previous pass's `-sm` workaround). Hero at 768 (0.6 of 1280): visible moiré banding in fdoc's foliage (`finalists-heroview.png`, top right). | Card: the grain drops below a screen pixel and reads as a grey photograph; the print character is gone. Hero: fine noise, clean. | The 16 px pitch is far above the pixel grid at every size, so the file resamples cleanly at 0.3 (card) and 0.6 (hero) with no moiré and no need for `image-rendering: pixelated`. Card: dots at ~5 px pitch, still visibly dots. Hero: 9.6 px pitch. |
| Engineered irregularity (4.6) | Integer shift of an 8×8 matrix, 512 distinct alignments; the shifted grid still looks like the same grid. | No periodic pattern to shift. | Continuous sub-cell shift of the lattice, visibly different per film on a flat field (`phase-check.png`); the 45° screen is itself off-axis against the page's rectilinear layout. |

Rejected, with the reason:

- **Floyd–Steinberg.** Fails the flattening question outright, and at card size
  it stops reading as a treatment at all. Handsome frame by frame, wrong for a
  grid whose point is that eleven inconsistent thumbnails read as one object.
- **Bayer 8×8.** The previous choice, and still a legitimate one on legibility.
  Rejected because (1) it reads as a filter rather than as print, and the
  crosshatch is the most machine-looking texture of the three, which is the
  exact failure mode the brief is about; (2) it moirés at the hero's 768 px
  width and needs the native-rendition workaround at card size, i.e. it only
  looks right at integer scales; (3) its irregularity is a shifted grid, not a
  different alignment. `bayer4` reads as crosshatch texture rather than a screen
  and is worse on every count; `atkinson` is the most graphic of the diffusions
  but blows fdoc's and omnes-unum's faces out to nothing (`approach-atkinson.png`).
- **Posterize before the screen** (`posterize.png`, 4 and 6 steps, both
  finalists). Dots come in only four or six sizes, which is a real risograph
  look, but fdoc's face becomes a flat carolina blob at 4 steps and the gain in
  print character is small at 6. Off by default; `--posterize=n` is kept for
  tuning.
- **Three tones** (`tones-3.png`, `deep` as a midtone). Every frame is smoother
  on its own and the set falls apart: the well-lit frames resolve into large
  flat `deep` fields and the grid shows who lit their film properly again, the
  same finding as the previous pass. Two tones.
- **Halftone pitch and working width** (`halftone-sweep.png`,
  `halftone-sweep-card.png`): 320/cell 6 and 320/cell 8 draw dots in 4 px
  blocks and they come out as crosses; 640/cell 10, 12 and 16 are rounder and
  more graphic but lose faces at the hero (cell 10 was the first candidate and
  was dropped on the `finalists-heroview.png` fdoc frame). 640/cell 8 is the
  finest pitch at which the dots are still round, and the one at which every
  face in the set reads at both sizes.

Screen angle is 45°, the single-ink convention and the angle at which a dot
lattice is least visible to the eye. `--angle` is exposed but was not swept.

## Engineered irregularity: the phase offset

Each film's screen is shifted by a phase derived from an FNV-1a 32-bit hash of
its slug (`phaseFor` in the script):

- **Halftone:** the lattice is translated by `(fx, fy)` cells, `fx` from bits
  9–19 and `fy` from bits 20–31 of the hash, each in `[0, 1)`, applied to the
  pixel coordinates before the 45° rotation. Because the dot is computed, not
  looked up, the shift is continuous and every film's dots sit at a different
  sub-cell position.
- **Bayer** (kept for comparison): an integer `(dx, dy)` shift of the threshold
  matrix from bits 0–5 plus one of its eight dihedral symmetries from bits 6–8,
  512 alignments.
- **Error diffusion:** no periodic pattern, so no phase is applied.

The hash is a pure function of the slug, so output depends only on the source
frame and the slug: two consecutive `npm run stills` runs produced 33
byte-identical files (md5 compared). Adding or removing a film never changes
another film's output. `--no-phase` disables it for A/B.

## Fallbacks and missing sources

with the 120×90 placeholder that YouTube serves for a missing max-res still
detected by width and skipped. Raw downloads are cached in `scripts/.cache/`
(gitignored) with a JSON sidecar recording the variant and size, so the
treatment can be re-tuned without re-fetching.

## Letterbox handling

Near-black rows are trimmed from the top and bottom (mean < 12 and max < 56 per
row, at most 30% from each edge), then the remainder is centre-cropped to 16:9.
Rows trimmed per film: fdoc 173, spaghetti-and-me 254, hard-pills-to-swallow
180, a-newby-cupids-guide 114, senior-assassin 44, slam 204 (the 4:3 sd frame
plus in-frame bars), the-tulips 2, everything else 0. discrete-magematics is
pillarboxed (a portrait poster on black); the script does not trim sides, since
cropping a 2:3 image to 16:9 would keep only a band across its middle.

## The known bad sources under the chosen treatment

Frames are not substituted (brief Part 8; design doc 17). How each fares:

- **discrete-magematics** (a 2:3 poster on black, not a frame). Treated, it is
  a solid carolina rectangle in a base field with the title in base. "DISCRETE"
  reads; "MAGEMATICS" is a smudge at card size and semi-legible at the hero; the
  wizard becomes a halftone silhouette. It is still the one card that visibly
  breaks the contact sheet, and it is the film's real thumbnail. The halftone
  handles it no better and no worse than Bayer did.
- **slam** (title card, sd source). The red "SLAM!" comes out as solid carolina
  letterforms with a halftoned figure behind; the dot pattern in the letter
  edges is the one place the sd softness shows. Reads as a poster, which is
  what it is.
- **the-tulips** (script title over a landscape). The title survives as
  carolina script; the barn and field become a dot gradient. Fine.
- **silenced** (baked-in "Official Selection, Argyle Film Festival 2026"
  laurel). The laurel survives the screen legibly, as it did under Bayer; it
  remains an award graphic outside the site's award system, which is a content
  question for the club, not a pipeline one. The face is a dot gradient under
  the hat; the "shh" gesture reads.
- **spaghetti-and-me** (very dark, face lit by a laptop). Normalise and gamma
  rescue it; the lamp, face and cardigan resolve as three dot fields against
  solid base. The sparsest frame and the best-looking one under this treatment.
- **fdoc** (face against busy foliage). The foliage becomes a halftone field
  rather than the noise it was under Bayer, and the face reads at card size and
  at the 768 px hero at pitch 16. This frame set the pitch: at 20 px the face
  went. It is the hero and the Best Picture still, so it was the deciding
  frame.

## Serving notes for the design system

- `{slug}-treated.webp` (1280×720) for the hero screen, the video facade and the
  awards finale; `{slug}-treated-sm.webp` (640×360) for film cards. Either
  resamples cleanly at any size with this treatment, so the split is a byte
  saving rather than a moiré fix.
- Do **not** set `image-rendering: pixelated` (or `crisp-edges`) on treated
  stills. At non-integer scales it turns the 2 px blocks into uneven 1–2 px
  steps and the round dots go ragged. Default smoothing is correct everywhere.
- Treated and original share dimensions per rendition, so the hover/focus
  crossfade cannot shift layout.
- Text over a treated still still needs a scrim (design doc 13); a 16 px dot
  field is as hostile to type as a 4 px one.

## Re-running

`npm run stills` is idempotent and reads from the cache; `--refetch` downloads
again; `--only=slug` limits the run; `--sheets` rebuilds every sheet above
(about 30 s). Adding a film to `films.json` and running `npm run stills` writes
its three renditions and leaves every other file byte-identical.
